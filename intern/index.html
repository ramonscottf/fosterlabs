§ 08

Amity's vetting (what's already in place)

Background checks happening regardless of your decision

Home-country police record

Every Amity intern must submit a clean police record from their home country. This is a hard requirement — required to issue the J-1 visa. Spanish background checks are robust.

US Embassy interview

Interns are screened by the US Embassy in Madrid during the J-1 visa interview. Independent layer of vetting from the State Department.

Amity Screening Officer interview

A regional Amity rep (often a Spanish university professor) does an in-person interview. They know the candidate's school and often personally.

School + district background check

Davis School District will run their own background check before she enters a Canyon Creek classroom. Standard for any adult working around children.

Translation

By the time she arrives in Utah she will have cleared four independent screening layers — Spanish police, US State Department, Amity's screening officer, and Davis School District. Whatever your host decision, those checks will happen.

§ 09

What to ask her on the Zoom call

Calibrated to surface the unresolved stuff
01
"Tell me about your Master's program — what are you studying?"
Open-ended. See if she says "Education" or "HR Management." Either is fine — but how she answers is the signal. A clean "I'm doing a Master's in Personnel Management, but for Amity I think of it as helping me develop the people skills that translate to teaching" is great. Evasion is not great.
02
"Your LinkedIn shows a strong luxury-brand and business track — Hermès thesis, INSIDE LVMH, the Madrid Chamber of Commerce. What made you decide to do the Amity year and a US elementary classroom instead of going straight into corporate?"
The single most important question. Listen for a real answer. Good answers: "I want my English perfect before I apply to BBVA / Santander corporate"; "I love kids and want to give back before adult life starts"; "I want a year of perspective before settling into a career." Stumbling, or pivoting away, is worth noting.
03
"When does the Master's program end? Will you have any coursework or thesis (TFM) trailing into the Fall when you'd be in Utah?"
This is the timeline wrinkle. Some Spanish Master's TFMs trail into the following academic year. A student trying to write a 70-page Master's thesis in your guest room while working a full-time classroom job is a recipe for burnout. Clean answer: "I'll finish in July before I leave."
04
"Tell me about your work at Bierzo Natura — what ages did you work with, and what was your role?"
Get specifics. Does she light up talking about kids? Does she have stories? Or does she give a generic "I worked with families and groups" answer? This is the closest match to what she'd be doing at Canyon Creek.
05
"In your application you mentioned that healthy food is very important to you — can you tell us more? Any dietary restrictions, allergies, or things you'd like us to know about meals?"
She wrote this twice in the application. Better to surface it upfront than to discover she's vegan or has a major aversion in week 2.
06
"How do you feel about Utah specifically? Have you been to the western US?"
She's from green, mountainous, wet northern Spain. Utah is high desert. The culture is also very specific. Worth gauging whether she has realistic expectations or whether she thinks she's coming to "California-ish America."
07
"Your LinkedIn shows you speak Italian — have you spent significant time abroad before?"
Italian fluency suggests Erasmus or extended Italy time. If she's done a semester or year abroad before, she's a known-good adapter and the homesickness risk drops significantly. If Italian is just from coursework or family, that's different.
08
"What are you most looking forward to about a US family? What are you most nervous about?"
Standard but vital. Listen for self-awareness in the "nervous" answer. A 22-year-old who can articulate what worries her about a year abroad is a 22-year-old who has thought it through.
09
"Do you have a driver's license? Are you planning to drive in the US?"
Amity generally discourages interns driving. Some districts prohibit it. Worth knowing upfront — affects transportation logistics significantly given Canyon Creek's location.
10
"Do you have questions for us? About our family, our home, Charles, Utah, anything?"
Always close with this. The questions she asks tell you what she actually cares about. A great candidate asks about Charles, about your routines, about what you'd want from her. A weaker candidate asks only about logistics (room, food, internet, transportation).
