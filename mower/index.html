Sand leveling: the curiosity that shouldn't drive the decision

Scott flagged "tractor for sand-leveling" as just curious. Honest answer: that's wise framing, because it's mostly a misconception.

Top-dressing a quarter-acre lawn with sand requires roughly 10–11 cubic yards (13–15 tons) for a meaningful 1/4–3/8" leveling pass — that's 2–3 dump truck loads. The actual work is shoveling sand from piles, distributing with a leveling rake, then dragging a mat to settle it into the canopy. A ride-on helps with exactly two pieces of that:

  1. Hauling sand in a dump cart from pile to spread location (lawn tractor with 250–650 lb cart helpful; ZTR limited)
  2. Pulling a drag mat across the spread sand to settle it (any ride-on works if you avoid zero-turn maneuvers)

The 80% of the work — shoveling, raking, walking — is unchanged. Pros use $4K–$15K powered topdressers (Ecolawn, Turfco) or skid-steers; homeowners use a Landzie leveling rake (~$90) which is the actual workhorse tool regardless of which mower you own. Don't pay $1,300 more for a tractor over a robot to gain a marginal sand-leveling assist on a project you'll do once every 3–5 years.

If Scott does want the towing utility for fertilizer/seed/aeration/hauling mulch — which is a more legitimate annual use — a lawn tractor wins easily over a zero-turn. Useful tractor accessories (any of the three picks above accept these):

  • Agri-Fab 130 lb tow broadcast spreader (~$220) — biggest real time saver, makes quarterly fertilization a 15-minute job
  • Brinly 36" lawn roller (~$250) + Yard Tuff 4.5'×5' drag mat (~$200) — for the leveling project he's curious about
  • 10 cu ft poly dump cart (~$200) — for hauling mulch, leaves, sand

Honda is dead — what to know about replacement walk-behinds

One last data point that affects Scott's mental model: Honda exited the US gas walk-behind mower market in September 2023. Remaining HRX217 inventory sold through 2024. As of 2026, you can only buy Honda walk-behinds used or new-old-stock. Parts/service still supported, but it's a sunsetting platform. The real successor for "premium 21" cut quality" is the Toro Super Recycler ($800–$1,100); for "I want to mow faster" it's the Toro TimeMaster 30" ($1,500–$1,850), which cuts mowing time roughly in half vs. a 21".

Conclusion: the ride-on isn't wrong, but it isn't right either

Scott's question was framed as "which ride-on?" — but the underlying job is "give me my time back so I can build." A ride-on partially solves that. A robot mower solves it better, cheaper, and with arguably better lawn quality on Kentucky bluegrass. A lawn service solves it best in pure time terms but costs $1,400/year forever and takes the lawn out of his hands.

The "tractor for sand leveling" curiosity is a small factor that shouldn't tilt a $1,300 spending decision. The slopes argument and "I'm new to ride-ons" argument do rule out zero-turns at this price tier — so if the answer is a ride-on, it's a lawn tractor, full stop, and the John Deere S120 is the cleanest pick.

The decision tree, in one sentence: If Scott wants maximum time back, buy the Husqvarna Automower 410iQ from Utah Robomowers in Kaysville. If Scott wants a ride-on because he wants a ride-on (which is a fine reason), buy the John Deere S120 at Home Depot Farmington and add the $650 accessory bundle. Either way, do not buy a zero-turn under $3K, do not buy a gear-driven tractor, and verify the actual lot size on the Davis County portal before pulling the trigger.
