How a draft works

It's a snake: pick order reverses each round (1→12, then 12→1). When it's your turn, you take one player. Roughly 15 rounds. Nobody can take a player twice — that's why you strike them off here.

The positions

QB throws. RB runs. WR catches. TE is a big guy who catches. DEF is a whole team's defense. K kicks. FLEX = one extra RB/WR/TE of your choice.

What "PPR" means

Point Per Reception — every catch is worth a point, plus yards and touchdowns. It makes pass-catching RBs and high-volume WRs extra valuable. This board is built for PPR.

The one big secret

Tiers beat rankings. Players in the same tier are basically equal — don't sweat which one. When a tier is nearly empty, grab from it before the drop-off. That's 80% of draft skill.

Golden rules — read once before the draft
  1. Rounds 1–5: only RBs and WRs. They score the most and run out fastest. Exception: if Josh Allen or Trey McBride/Brock Bowers falls to you in round 3–4, that's fine value.
  2. One QB, one TE. Ever. Don't take a second of either until your bench is full of RB/WR. Waiting on QB is smart — the QB8 scores almost as much as the QB3.
  3. Kicker and Defense in the last two rounds. Anyone who takes them earlier is donating value. If you remember only one rule, this is it.
  4. Best player available > filling a need in the early rounds. Roster holes fix themselves later.
  5. Don't reach for names you recognize. Travis Kelce and Patrick Mahomes are famous, not early picks anymore. Trust the board.
  6. Byes barely matter. The "Bye" number is the one week that player sits out. Only avoid stacking if your top 3–4 picks all share the same bye week.

Table talk: "sleeper" = underrated player, "bust" = overrated, "handcuff" = the backup to your star RB, "ADP" = where players usually get drafted. Nod knowingly.

Round-by-Round Plan

R1–2 Anchor

Take the best RB or WR on the board. If you pick early, that's Gibbs, Bijan, or Nacua. Don't overthink it — everyone in the Elite tier is a great pick.

R3–5 Stack the skill spots

Keep hammering RB/WR until you have 2 of each minimum. If Josh Allen, Trey McBride, or Brock Bowers is still sitting there in R4–5, they're worth the detour.

R6–9 QB + TE window

Grab your one QB and one TE here if you haven't. Burrow, Lamar, Dak, Jayden Daniels are all fine — same tier, don't chase. TE thins out fast after Loveland/Warren.

R10–13 Bench depth & upside

More RB/WR. Prefer young players and backups to stars over old veterans — bench players only matter if they break out or someone gets hurt.

R14–15 DEF, then K

Seattle or Denver defense if available, otherwise any defense. Kicker with your literal last pick — genuinely any kicker. There is no wrong kicker.
