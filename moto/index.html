The Cram Sheet · Part 1

Numbers they love to test

Braking

  • Front brake = at least 70% of stopping power
  • Use BOTH brakes every time you slow or stop
  • Front wheel locks → release immediately, reapply firmly
  • Rear wheel locks (straight, good traction) → keep it locked until stopped
  • Quick stop in a curve → straighten the bike first, then brake hard

Space & Vision

  • Following distance: 2 seconds minimum — 3+ if slippery/heavy traffic
  • Scan your path 12 seconds ahead; 4-second zone = immediate
  • SEE = Search, Evaluate, Execute
  • Signal 2 seconds before any turn or lane change UT
  • Most crashes: short trips under 5 miles, speeds under 30 mph

Riders & Crashes

  • Over half of crashes: riders with < 5 months experience
  • Over half of motorcycle/car crashes: drivers violating the rider's right-of-way
  • #1 danger spot: intersections — especially left-turning cars
  • 1 in 5 crashes = head/neck injury; helmets = 3x more likely to survive a head injury
  • Crash-involved riders typically underbrake the front, overbrake the rear

Alcohol

  • Utah legal limit: 0.05% — the only state that low UT
  • Under 21: essentially zero tolerance (0.00–0.02)
  • Body eliminates ~1 drink per hour — beer = shot = wine glass
  • Judgment goes first — you feel better while riding worse
  • ~1/3 of fatally injured riders were over the limit; even 1–2 drinks impairs
The Cram Sheet · Part 2

How to ride (per the test)

Turning

  • Sequence: SLOW → LOOK → PRESS → ROLL
  • Countersteer: press LEFT grip = lean left = GO LEFT
  • Look through the turn — turn just your head, not shoulders
  • Normal turns: lean WITH the bike. Slow tight turns: bike leans, you stay upright
  • Roll on throttle through the turn — it stabilizes the bike

Lane Strategy

  • Every lane = 3 positions; none is forbidden — choose to see and be seen
  • Center strip: oil/debris — but usually OK traction unless wet
  • Rain: ride in the tire tracks left by cars
  • Behind a car: ride where the driver sees you in the rearview mirror
  • Never linger in blind spots; make head checks before lane changes
  • Passing parked cars: left portion of your lane (doors!)

Emergencies

  • Swerve = press the grip on the side you want to escape
  • Never brake while swerving — brake before OR after
  • Wobble: grip firmly, don't fight it, roll OFF throttle gradually — no brakes
  • Flat tire: hold grips firmly, ease off throttle, keep straight; brake the good tire if you know which
  • Stuck throttle: twist several times → still stuck? engine cut-off + clutch in
  • Chased by a dog: approach slowly, then accelerate away
  • Big animals (deer): brake and prepare to stop. Small animal in traffic: stay in your lane

Surfaces

  • Crossing tracks: usually ride straight in your lane (angling to 90° can take you into traffic)
  • Parallel tracks/seams: cross at 45° or more, deliberate turn
  • Rain grooves/bridge grates: relax, steady speed, straight across — wandering is normal
  • Slippery: gentle everything; front brake still works — squeeze, never grab
  • Obstacle you must hit: 90° angle, slow, rise off seat, slight throttle just before contact

Passengers & Cargo

  • Passenger: lean as you lean, feet on pegs even when stopped, sit close/forward, look over your shoulder in turns
  • Children: behind the rider, never in front
  • Mount only after engine is started, transmission in neutral
  • With a passenger: ride slower, slow earlier, bigger gaps
  • Loads: low, over/in front of rear axle, even saddlebags, bungees not rope

Group Riding

  • Staggered formation; single file in curves, turns, entering/leaving highways
  • Inexperienced riders ride just behind the leader
  • Lead + sweep = most experienced riders
  • Never pair up side-by-side in one lane
  • Pass one at a time on two-lane roads; no passing within the group

Be Seen

  • Headlight ON always — twice as likely to be noticed in daylight
  • Bright/reflective gear — day AND night
  • Flash your brake light before slowing suddenly or where unexpected, and when stopped with traffic behind
  • Never rely on eye contact — it doesn't mean they'll yield
  • Use signals every time, then cancel them; horn = quick beep before passing
  • Cover clutch + both brakes in high-risk zones to cut reaction time

Fit To Ride

  • Gear: DOT helmet, face/eye protection (windshield is NOT a substitute), jacket+pants, boots over ankles, gloves
  • Faceshield beats goggles — protects your whole face
  • No tinted lenses in low light
  • Fatigue: max ~6 hours riding/day, break every 2 hours
  • Pre-ride check = T-CLOCS: Tires, Controls, Lights, Oil, Chassis, Stands
The Cram Sheet · Part 3

Utah-only rules (guaranteed questions)

Helmets: required by law only under age 21 (DOT-approved). Everyone's encouraged; 21+ wearing DOT headgear even gets $8 waived off a moving-violation fine.
Tiered licensing: you're restricted to the size class you test on: ≤90cc, ≤249cc, ≤649cc, or 650cc+ = unrestricted. Test on a 250? You're in the ≤649 tier.
Learner permit (first 2 months): no highways posted 60+ mph, no passengers, no riding 10 PM–6 AM. Restrictions lift after 2 months. Under 19 must hold the permit 2 months (waivable via the Basic Rider Course). Permit valid 6 months; requires valid license + vision + written test.
Lane filtering is LEGAL — but only when ALL are true: road has 2+ lanes same direction, speed limit ≤45 mph (never on-ramps), vehicles you pass are STOPPED, you go ≤15 mph, and it's safe. Everything else between lanes = illegal.
Dead red light rule: age 16+, complete stop, signal hasn't detected you after a reasonable wait (at least 90 seconds) → you may proceed when clear.
Equipment: ONE mirror required (not two). Turn signals required on bikes made after Jan 1, 1973. Handlebars no higher than shoulder height. Tread ≥ 2/32". Passenger footrests required to carry a passenger. No safety inspections since 2018.
Also: motorcycles get full use of a lane and may use HOV lanes; max two abreast per lane; insurance evidence must be in your possession; implied consent to chemical tests; register within 60 days of residency; address change reported within 10 days. Utah's top crash causes: failure to yield, following too closely, improper lane, speed for conditions, ran off road.
